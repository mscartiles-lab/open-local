Include these sections as expandable accordion cards:

Florida Cottage Food Law (§500.80) — no permit needed, $250K cap, shelf-stable only, can sell online/at markets/from home, no wholesale, home kitchen only, Florida sales only
Allowed cottage foods — baked goods, jams/jellies (high-acid fruit), honey, syrups, roasted nuts, granola, candy, fudge, dry mixes, pasta, nut butters, dried fruits
Prohibited cottage foods — anything requiring refrigeration (TCS foods), meat/poultry/seafood, cut fruits/vegetables, raw sprouts, home-canned goods, dairy as standalone, pet food
Required product labeling — seller name and home address, product name, ingredient list by weight, net weight/volume, allergen warnings, required statement in 10pt contrasting font: "Made in a cottage food operation that is not subject to Florida's food safety regulations."
FDACS permit — for pre-packaged non-cottage foods, refrigerated/frozen products, wholesale/retail businesses, mobile vendors; apply at foodpermit.fdacs.gov
DBPR license — for ready-to-eat on-site foods, food trucks, hot food/smoothies/juice; fees: 1-3 days ~$91, 4-30 days ~$105, annual ~$456; apply at myfloridalicense.com
Universal requirements — Florida sales tax registration (850-488-6800), local business license/occupational permit (check city/county), general liability insurance, DBA registration at sunbiz.org, event permits
Quick reference table — Home baker → None (Cottage Food exempt) · Packaged honey/granola → None · Farm with refrigerated items → FDACS · Food truck/hot food → DBPR · Lemonade/smoothies → DBPR · Candles/crafts/jewelry → None
